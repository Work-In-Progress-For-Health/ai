# Spec: PADR (Performance Appraisal and Development Review) facts

This file is the single source of truth for facts about the PADR process used in README.md. If README.md and this file ever disagree, this file is correct, and README.md needs updating to match it.

## What a PADR is

A PADR (Performance Appraisal and Development Review) is the structured evaluation and career-planning process used across NHS Wales health boards and organisations, including DHCW. It is designed as a continuous feedback mechanism, not a once-a-year form, with two core components:

- Performance Appraisal — reviewing past performance, evaluating behaviours, and setting measurable future objectives.
- Personal Development Plan (PDP) — identifying skills gaps, mapping out necessary training, and supporting long-term career progression.

## Tie to pay progression

In many NHS Wales organisations, a PADR is a mandatory prerequisite for salary increments under standard pay progression frameworks. A manager or POD/HR reviewer uses the PADR meeting to verify that an employee has met their core objectives, demonstrated the expected behavioural values, and completed required mandatory training, before a pay increase is authorised.

## Variation by organisation

Exact PADR forms, scheduling, and how tightly they're tied to pay progression vary between individual NHS Wales health boards and organisations. This project treats the general shape above (continuous, two-part, tied to pay progression) as reliable across NHS Wales, and treats organisation-specific procedural detail as something to confirm directly with a POD/HR contact or an organisation's staff intranet — not something this project asserts on their behalf.

## Relationship to the OCP

The OCP's "development review/personal development plan process" (OCP §4.2 — see [`spec/index.md`](index.md)) is the same mechanism as the PDP half of a PADR. This project treats a PADR as the named, formal vehicle through which an NHS Wales employee's OCP-derived training rights are meant to be exercised, not a separate or optional process.

## Terminology note

"PADR" is also, rarely and in unrelated contexts, an initialism for other things (e.g. an EU defence-research programme, a disaster-risk-assessment framework). None of those meanings apply in this project; every use of "PADR" here means Performance Appraisal and Development Review.
